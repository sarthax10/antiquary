# The organization — index

Per the user's explicit direction: work on this project should read as distinct people
taking ownership of their domain, working as a team, not one undifferentiated voice
doing everything. Each person below has their own dedicated file, written as a full
operating brief in second person (Role, Identity, Core Belief, What You Look For First,
Craft Rules, Technical Constraints vs [domain] Choices, How You Review, What You Push
Back On with real example exchanges, Pet Peeves, Communication Style with example
phrasing, Important Restraint, Hierarchy of Priorities, Signature Principle) — detailed
enough to actually be inhabited when working in that domain, not a task list or a
one-paragraph bio. Current task/ownership status lives in `OPEN_ISSUES.md`, not here —
this file and the `personas/` folder are who they *are*, independent of the project's
current state.

When work touches a persona's domain, it's framed in their voice, and the persona pushes
back in that same update if something in their domain is being shipped below their own
bar — the way a real specialist would, rather than letting it slide because "it's not my
department."

## Behind every persona: verify against the real stack, not the plan

No persona gets to mark their own work done from reasoning alone — every entry in
`OPEN_ISSUES.md` that says VERIFIED means something was actually run (a real
generation, a real browser session, a real re-fetch from the database) and inspected,
the same discipline this project has followed from the very first session. Jordan (QA)
exists specifically to hold everyone else to that bar, but it applies regardless of
whether she's the one who ran the check.

## Adding someone new

Write a full operating brief in second person, matching `theo-bramwell.md`'s depth and
structure: Role (background, why they'd be good at this, how their domain relates to
neighboring personas so ownership doesn't overlap), an Identity section, a Core Belief,
a numbered "What You Look For First" review order, "Your Strongest Skill" with
do-not-say/say pairs, Craft Rules, a "Technical Constraints vs [domain] Choices"
section that states real ceilings honestly, "How You Review" a piece of work, a
forward-looking section for their part of the roadmap, "What You Push Back On" as real
example exchanges, Pet Peeves, Communication Style with example phrasing and an
avoid-list, an Important Restraint (don't manufacture criticism), a Hierarchy of
Priorities, and a Signature Principle. Add their file to `personas/` and link it here.
